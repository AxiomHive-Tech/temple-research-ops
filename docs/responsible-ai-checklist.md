# Axiom Hive Technology: Responsible AI and Relationship Reliability Checklist

**Purpose:** Support businesses and individuals in keeping business development and client, employee, and customer relationships reliable through responsible research, design, deployment, and operation of automated systems.

**Authored by:** Caden McCullen  
**Technology leadership and engineering founders (as provided):** Nicholas Michael Grossi and Alexis M. Adam’s

Use this checklist when proposing, building, purchasing, deploying, or materially changing an AI-enabled workflow. A checked box records that the item was addressed; it does not by itself prove that the system is safe or appropriate.

## 1. Responsibility remains human

- [ ] Name the accountable business owner and the technical owner. Make clear that automation does not transfer legal, ethical, or operational responsibility away from people or the organization.
- [ ] Define which decisions the system may recommend, automate, or must escalate to a qualified person.
- [ ] Provide a human review and correction path for consequential or disputed outcomes.
- [ ] Define how incidents, mistakes, complaints, and suspected misuse are reported, investigated, and remedied.
- [ ] Prohibit using automated systems to conceal wrongdoing, destroy evidence, mislead affected people, or evade accountability.

## 2. Research before design

- [ ] Describe the problem, the people affected, the current workflow, and the outcome to improve before selecting a technology.
- [ ] Compare non-AI and lower-risk alternatives. State why automation is appropriate for this use case.
- [ ] Convert needs into testable requirements, acceptance criteria, and known constraints.
- [ ] Identify affected groups, including clients, employees, customers, and people whose data may be processed.
- [ ] Test assumptions with representative users and realistic scenarios before broad deployment.

## 3. Keep a traceable evidence record

- [ ] For each material claim or design decision, record the source, date, relevance, method, and limitations.
- [ ] Separate statements into **Fact**, **Estimate**, **Interpretation**, or **Hypothesis**; identify the basis for estimates and interpretations.
- [ ] Record major decisions, their rationale, alternatives considered, known risks, and the person approving them.
- [ ] Preserve links or identifiers for evidence and note the version of relevant data, models, prompts, policies, or configuration.
- [ ] Do not present undocumented or opaque training data as independently verified evidence. State what is known and what cannot be assessed.

## 4. Examine disagreement, bias, and impact

- [ ] Record credible sources or stakeholder perspectives that disagree; do not force a false consensus.
- [ ] Investigate whether differences arise from population, context, time period, definitions, measurement, or methodology.
- [ ] Evaluate potential disparate impacts and foreseeable harmful bias using evidence appropriate to the use case.
- [ ] Distinguish what the team intended from how the system and its actions affected people.
- [ ] In ambiguous situations, require evidence before attributing hostile motives. Describe observable behavior and uncertainty respectfully.
- [ ] Document unresolved conflicts and the conditions that would trigger further investigation or a pause.

## 5. Be transparent with affected people

- [ ] Explain when AI is used, what it does, what information it uses, and where its limits matter to a person’s decision or relationship.
- [ ] Provide a clear way to reach a responsible human, challenge an outcome, correct information, or request follow-up.
- [ ] Disclose material limitations, uncertainty, conflicts of interest, and relevant data or model provenance where available.
- [ ] Do not claim a system is objective, unbiased, or fully explainable without evidence supporting that claim.

## 6. Test and monitor before and after deployment

- [ ] Define success, failure, and harm indicators before launch; include relationship-quality indicators such as complaint resolution, correction time, and escalation outcomes where relevant.
- [ ] Test normal, edge, adversarial, and failure scenarios with representative and privacy-safe data.
- [ ] Confirm privacy, security, access control, retention, and data deletion requirements for the workflow.
- [ ] Start with a limited rollout when feasible, with a human fallback and a documented rollback or shutdown path.
- [ ] Assign an owner to review logs and feedback. Log evidence needed for audit while minimizing sensitive data and controlling access.
- [ ] Reassess after meaningful changes to models, prompts, data, vendors, workflow, or user population.

## 7. Accept feedback and repair harm

- [ ] Provide a respectful and accessible channel for feedback from affected people and staff.
- [ ] Respond to concerns specifically: identify the behavior or outcome, the evidence reviewed, what remains uncertain, and what action will follow.
- [ ] Track corrective actions, owners, deadlines, and completion evidence.
- [ ] When harm or a significant error occurs, prioritize containment, notification where appropriate, correction, and learning over defending the system.
